Data as Material · PSAM 5170 · Spring 2027

Making a Project

Every project in this course runs through the same loop. The data is your material, and the form you give it is your argument.

Three ideas to hold on to

Question before medium

Know what you want to find out or say before you pick p5.js, plywood, or a model. If you can only describe the tool, you don't have a concept yet.

The translation is the work

Every choice about how data becomes form (scale, color, material, order, sound) carries meaning. Make those choices on purpose and be able to explain them.

Prototype to answer a question

A prototype tests one thing. Hand it to someone and say "try it." A slide deck about your idea is not a prototype.

The loop

  1. 01

    Question

    Concept

    What data, and why? What are you curious about, and what might it show that you didn't know?

    Who collected this data, and what did they leave out? Find 2–3 precedents and say how yours is different. Bring: a few sentences on your concept and your precedents.

  2. 02

    Sketch

    Form & material

    How will the data become form? Start on paper.

    Write your rules: which part of the data drives which property. Name 2–3 qualities you want people to feel (e.g. quiet, urgent, intimate). Bring: sketches and your mapping rules.

  3. 03

    Prototype & test

    Experience

    Can someone read it? Do your qualities come across? What do people actually do with it?

    Make it fast and cheap, then test it with someone, ideally from outside this class. Watch before you explain. Bring: the prototype and what you learned. Read: Houde & Hill, What Do Prototypes Prototype?

  4. 04

    Make

    Implementation

    Build it with the real tools and fold in the crit feedback.

    The finished piece should make sense without you standing next to it. Instructions are fine; a backstory shouldn't be required. If a technical piece won't work in time, solve it with design and presentation.

  5. 05

    Document

    Communication

    Could a stranger understand the project from your page alone?

    On your GitHub Pages site: concept, data source, mapping rules, process images (sketches, failed tests too), final images or video, and what you'd change. Name every dataset, model, and tool you used.

What the translation is in each project

01

Remix

A text you didn't write, given 20 forms. Content stays, form changes.

02

Track or Feed

Your own data or a live feed, drawn on paper first, then on screen.

03

Physical Data

A dataset given material form. The material's associations carry meaning too.

04

Living Archive

An archive run through ML to make a new dataset, then a visualization of what it shows.

05

Data Experience

AI systems used to make something people move through, use, or take part in.

Before crit, can you answer these?

  • Concept. What is the project about, in one sentence, without naming the tool?
  • Design. Why does it look, feel, or sound this way? What would change if you changed the mapping?
  • Communication. What do you want people to notice, and does the work show it without you explaining?
  • Technical. Does your data translation method work, and can you show how you built it?

The four checks match the rubric categories in the syllabus.
